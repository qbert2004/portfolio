Power BI · Task 01 · 2025

Military Expenditure
% of GDP

Overview

Built a multi-visual Power BI dashboard analyzing global military spending as a percentage of GDP across 100+ countries. The dashboard integrates a world map, pie chart, bar chart ranking, and an AI-powered Q&A panel to enable dynamic exploration of the data.

Key Findings
  • Ukraine's military spending rose sharply in 2014–2015, coinciding with the annexation of Crimea and the start of armed conflict in eastern Ukraine.
  • From 2014 onward, Ukraine shows a clear structural break and a sustained upward trend, with particularly strong acceleration after 2021–2022.
  • After 2014–2015, U.S. military spending initially stagnated or slightly declined, and then started to increase again in the late 2010s.
  • Oman and Saudi Arabia consistently rank among the highest spenders as a share of GDP, reflecting regional security dynamics.
  • The AI Q&A panel enables on-demand analysis, showing country-by-year rankings sorted by total value as a treemap.

Ireland: Military &
Economic Dashboard

Overview

Interactive Power BI dashboard analyzing Ireland's military expenditure, high-tech exports, FDI inflows, and GDP growth from 1960 to 2024. Multi-layered time-series visualizations reveal the structural economic transformation of Ireland over six decades.

0.24%Military Expenditure / GDP

Key Findings
  • Ireland maintains one of the lowest military expenditure rates in the EU at 0.24% of GDP, reflecting its traditional policy of military neutrality.
  • High-tech exports show a dramatic rise from 2000 onward, reflecting Ireland's emergence as a European hub for multinational technology corporations.
  • FDI inflows peaked in the early 2000s during the Celtic Tiger period and show significant volatility linked to global financial cycles.
  • GDP growth of 2.60% in 2024 reflects continued economic resilience despite housing pressures and EU regulatory constraints.
  • The divergence between military expenditure and high-tech export growth confirms Ireland's strategy of economic security over military investment.

Diplomatic Writing · Task 04 · 2026

Paris Protest Security

U.S. DEPARTMENT OF STATE
BUREAU OF SOUTH AND CENTRAL ASIAN AFFAIRS
BRIEFING NOTE — CONFIDENTIAL
Subject

Security risk assessment for diplomatic personnel and U.S. interests in Paris, France, during an escalating period of government reform protests. Produced in formal U.S. Department of State briefing note format.

Methodology
  • OSINT monitoring across BBC, France24, Twitter/X, Reddit, and Telegram protest channels.
  • AI-assisted scenario forecasting using probabilistic risk modelling.
  • Structured analytical frameworks adapted from U.S. State Department reporting standards.
  • Cross-referencing with historical protest escalation patterns in Western European democracies.
Key Recommendations
  • Enhanced situational awareness protocols for diplomatic staff in central Paris arrondissements.
  • Temporary avoidance of demonstration convergence zones on designated protest days.
  • Maintained communication channels with French Ministry of Interior and Prefecture de Police.
  • Continuous OSINT monitoring to detect escalation indicators including crowd size, police response, and social media sentiment shifts.
RISK ASSESSMENT

Medium-level security risk. Protests are constitutionally protected and police-managed. No immediate threat to diplomatic facilities. Recommend heightened awareness over a 30-day monitoring window.

Political Analysis · Task 05 · 2026

Briefing Note:
Ireland Socio-Economic

DIPLOMATIC INTELLIGENCE REPORT
IRELAND — CIVIC STABILITY ASSESSMENT
OSINT-BASED · RESTRICTED DISTRIBUTION
Subject

Assessment of emerging civic instability in Ireland, covering three primary protest vectors: agricultural sector demonstrations, cost-of-living marches in Dublin, and Shannon Airport activist proceedings related to U.S. military transit permissions.

Protest Vectors Analyzed
  • Agricultural protests — driven by EU nitrates directive and land-use regulations disproportionately affecting Irish farmers. Organized through IFA and ICMSA networks.
  • Cost-of-living demonstrations — urban-focused unrest linked to Ireland's acute housing crisis, rising rents, and insufficient residential construction pipeline.
  • Shannon Airport proceedings — civil society challenge to government policy allowing U.S. military aircraft transit, raising questions about Irish neutrality.
OSINT Sources
  • Media monitoring: BBC, The Irish Times, RTE, The Journal, Reuters.
  • Social media: Twitter/X protest hashtags, Facebook event tracking, Telegram community channels.
  • Government sources: Dail Eireann debates, Department of Agriculture statements, An Garda Siochana briefings.
FORECAST

Low-intensity protracted instability. No systemic threat to political institutions. Recommended: constructive stakeholder engagement, enhanced OSINT monitoring, and proactive government communication strategy to prevent escalation.
